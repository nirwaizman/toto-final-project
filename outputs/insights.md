## 📌 Key Business Insights

- **City Hotel carries a dangerously high cancellation rate of 41.8%** — compared to just 28.1% for Resort Hotel. With over 4 in 10 city hotel bookings never materialising into an actual stay, the overbooking policy must be recalibrated urgently. A conservative overbooking buffer of at least 15–20% is warranted for City Hotel, while Resort Hotel's lower 28.1% rate allows for a more measured approach. Treating both hotels with the same overbooking policy is a revenue management mistake.

- **The overall cancellation rate of 37.3% across 117,567 bookings represents a structural revenue leakage problem.** At an average daily rate of ~€101 blended, even a 1% reduction in cancellations could recover tens of thousands of euros in realised revenue annually. Predictive cancellation modelling is not a nice-to-have — it is a financial imperative.

- **City Hotel commands a €10.03/night price premium over Resort Hotel (€105.51 vs. €95.48 ADR), yet this premium is undermined by its 41.8% cancellation rate.** The higher ADR creates an illusion of stronger revenue performance, but realised revenue per booking is significantly eroded by no-shows. Resort Hotel's lower ADR (€95.48) paired with a 28.1% cancellation rate may yield more predictable and stable net revenue per booking slot.

- **Portugal is the dominant guest origin market with 47,784 bookings — nearly 4× the next largest market, the United Kingdom (12,100 bookings).** Western Europe (UK, France, Spain, Germany, Italy, Ireland, Belgium, Netherlands) collectively forms the core international demand base. Brazil (2,220 bookings) stands out as the only non-European market in the top 10, signalling an emerging South American feeder segment worth targeted marketing investment.

- **Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) dominate the booking channel mix at 47.9% (56,318 bookings), creating a significant third-party dependency risk.** Combined with Offline TA/TO at 20.3% (23,867 bookings), over 68% of all bookings flow through intermediaries that typically charge 15–25% commission. With Direct bookings at only 10.5% (12,354) and Corporate at 4.3% (5,084), there is a clear strategic opportunity to invest in direct channel growth to reduce commission costs and improve margin.

- **The average booking lead time of 104.6 days (~3.5 months) provides a meaningful window for dynamic pricing and yield management interventions.** Cancellation risk models trained on lead time data can flag high-risk bookings early enough to trigger targeted retention actions (e.g., non-refundable rate incentives, deposit requirements). Bookings made far in advance with flexible rates should be treated as higher cancellation risk and priced or policy-managed accordingly.

- **The Groups segment (16.1%, 18,987 bookings) represents a concentrated risk block.** Group bookings tend to be large-volume, single-decision-maker contracts — a single group cancellation can vacate multiple rooms simultaneously. Combined with OTA dependency, this means the hotel's revenue base is exposed to both high-frequency individual cancellations and high-impact group cancellations. Diversification toward Corporate (currently only 4.3%) would reduce both commission costs and cancellation volatility.

- **The dataset originates from Portuguese hotels, and Portugal's outsized share (47,784 bookings vs. 12,100 for the UK in second place) confirms a strong domestic/near-market demand base.** Revenue strategies should prioritise retention and loyalty programmes for Portuguese guests while actively developing the UK, French, Spanish, and German markets — the four largest international segments — through targeted OTA listing optimisation and direct marketing campaigns.

## ⚠️ Data Leakage Warning

> **The following columns MUST NOT be used as model features under any circumstances.**

| Column | Reason |
|---|---|
| `reservation_status` | This column directly encodes the outcome (e.g., "Canceled", "Check-Out", "No-Show"). Using it as a feature would constitute **target leakage** — the model would be learning the answer, not predicting it. Any model trained with this column will show artificially perfect accuracy and fail completely in production. |
| `reservation_status_date` | This is the date on which the reservation status was last updated — it is a **post-event timestamp** that is only known *after* the cancellation or check-out has occurred. Including it as a feature leaks future information into the model at training time, making it unavailable at the point of real-world prediction. |

**These two columns must be explicitly excluded from the feature matrix (`X`) before any model training, cross-validation, or feature importance analysis. The dataset contract below enforces this programmatically.**
