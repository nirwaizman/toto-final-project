Block 1
Meet Toto. A small AI assistant that runs an entire data team, turning messy hotel bookings into predictions before anyone checks in.
Block 2
Here is the problem. More than one in three hotel bookings gets canceled. Empty rooms, wasted staff, and revenue lost every single day.
Block 3
This project simulates a real AI product team. Seven specialized agents work in two crews and hand work to each other automatically.
Block 4
Crew one is the data analyst. Its first agent loads over one hundred nineteen thousand real bookings and profiles every single column.
Block 5
A cleaning agent fixes missing values and drops broken rows. Then an analyst digs in: city hotels cancel forty two percent of bookings.
Block 6
The last analyst writes business insights and a formal data contract, a machine readable promise about what the data really contains.
Block 7
Now the flow steps in. A validation gate checks the contract against the real data. If anything is wrong, it stops right there.
Block 8
Crew two is the data scientist. It validates the contract again, then builds features, carefully leaving out anything known only after the outcome.
Block 9
Two models compete. Random Forest against Gradient Boosting, trained on twenty fifteen and sixteen, then tested on twenty seventeen.
Block 10
Gradient Boosting wins, with an area under the curve of point eight seven five. Every metric is written into a transparent report.
Block 11
A model card explains what the model is for, its limits, and its ethics. Support planning decisions, never judge an individual guest.
Block 12
Finally, a Streamlit app runs the whole flow and serves live predictions. That is Toto. Specialized agents, verified handoffs, real decisions.
