Block 1
A hotel group collects thousands of bookings every month, but nobody could answer one question: which reservations were about to be canceled.

Block 2
So we built Toto, an AI system made of two collaborating crews, each one handling a different half of the problem.

Block 3
The first crew, four data analyst agents, ingested one hundred nineteen thousand real hotel bookings from Portugal.

Block 4
One agent profiled the raw data, another cleaned it, a third explored the patterns, and a fourth wrote the business insights.

Block 5
They discovered that city hotel bookings canceled forty two percent of the time, nearly double the resort hotel rate.

Block 6
Before handing off, the team wrote a strict data contract, flagging which columns would leak the answer and must never be used.

Block 7
An automated flow checked that contract against the cleaned data, refusing to continue if anything did not match.

Block 8
Only then did the second crew begin, three data scientist agents ready to build a real predictive model.

Block 9
They engineered clean features, trained two competing models, and compared them honestly on unseen future bookings.

Block 10
The winning model reached eighty seven point six percent accuracy at telling which bookings were truly at risk.

Block 11
Every decision was documented in a model card, listing its purpose, its limits, and its ethical boundaries.

Block 12
This is what real collaboration between AI agents looks like: validated, transparent, and built to be trusted.
